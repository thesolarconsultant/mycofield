2. What MycoField is, and what it is not

  • MycoField combines public weather, soil-moisture, terrain and official habitat data into an experimental Conditions Index for grassland fungi in general. The index is not a probability and has not been scientifically validated. It can be wrong.
  • It does not identify fungi and gives no advice on whether anything is edible or safe. Many fungi are deadly. Never eat a wild mushroom unless an expert has identified it in person.
  • Third-party data (for example Open-Meteo, Natural Resources Wales, Natural England, the Rural Payments Agency, NatureScot and OpenStreetMap) can be incomplete, out of date or temporarily unavailable. We show where each figure comes from but cannot guarantee it.

3. Out in the field

You are responsible for your own safety and for following the law and the Countryside Code (or the Scottish Outdoor Access Code). In particular: get the landowner’s permission where you need it, follow byelaws on nature reserves and Sites of Special Scientific Interest, don’t pick protected species, and look, photograph and record rather than collect. MycoField shows public data; it does not give you a right of access to any land.
